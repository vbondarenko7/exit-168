The sauna is right on the river

  1. Wood stove with a water tank on the stones
  2. You light it. Firewood is stacked and ready.
  3. The stones heat up while you unpack
  4. Sweat it out on the two-tier benches
  5. Down the steps into the river, then back in

    It's the South Yuba, cold snowmelt. People swim here from mid-July through October. During spring runoff, May to early July, the river is dangerous and nobody swims. In winter it's a quick plunge and straight back into the sauna, and only if the river is calm that day.

The cabin sleeps 12

  • Bunk room with a hand-built triple bunk, a bedroom and a sleeping loft
  • Kitchen with a gas stove, fridge and microwave, and a big carved dining table
  • Living room with a wood stove, a big sectional and a foosball table
  • Two decks with tables, benches and grills
  • Indoor bathroom with a flush toilet, a shower and hot water
  • Solar power and a generator. No Wi-Fi, and cell service is spotty.

How a stay works

  1. Getting there

    Summer and fall, you drive right up to the cabin. The road is paved from the gate, no 4WD needed. In winter the road isn't plowed. Park by the road near the gate and snowshoe the last two-thirds of a mile or so.

  2. When you arrive

    The cabin and sauna are clean and the firewood is stacked. You light the sauna stove and the living-room wood stove yourselves. Never leave a fire unattended. If anything is dirty or broken, send us a photo within the first hour.

  3. Before you leave

    Wash the dishes, sweep the cabin and the sauna, clean the grills and put the furniture back. Take all trash and leftover food with you.

  4. Check-out

    Check-out is at 10 a.m. Lock up and leave the key where we tell you. Your security deposit comes back within 7 days, minus the cost of anything broken, lost or left needing extra cleaning.

What to bring

Food and drinks
Bring everything for the group. The nearest big grocery store is in Truckee, 20 minutes away. Bears live here, so keep food and anything scented inside the cabin, and never leave food in the car overnight.
Sheets and towels
Bring your own sheets, bedding and towels for everyone in the group.
Drinking water
The tap water may not be safe to drink, so bring drinking water for the whole stay.

Yoga and retreats for up to 15

We think of this as a place to be alone with nature, and it works well for yoga and retreats. Groups of up to 15 can stay a few days at a time. There's no internet. You bring the teacher, the mats and the schedule. Practice on the deck, then go to the sauna and the river after.

Hiking to granite lakes

The Loch Leven Lakes trail starts 1 mile from the exit. It's about 3.5 miles uphill to the lakes, usually from June to November. The Pacific Crest Trail crosses I-80 at Donner Summit, 15 minutes east. Stay 3 nights or more and you can also explore the 168 acres right from the cabin.

Trout in the South Yuba

The South Yuba has brown and rainbow trout. California trout season runs from late April to mid-November, and anyone 16 or older needs a California fishing license.

Hunting with 3+ nights

With a stay of 3 nights or more, you can hunt in California's open seasons with your own California hunting license and tags. Non-lead ammo only, and state and county law applies. No shooting within 150 yards of any building, or from, across or toward a road. No shooting toward I-80, the railroad or the river. No baiting. No gut piles within 150 ft of the river. Loaded guns stay out of the cabin and sauna. No guns with alcohol, no target shooting. Hunters sign a waiver and need insurance. We don't guide hunts.

Bring your own off-road vehicles

Stay 3 nights or more and you can bring your own dirt bikes, quads (ATVs), snowmobiles and 4x4s or Jeeps. Let us know ahead of time what you're bringing. Stick to the routes we show you, and stay out of closed areas. Working mufflers, helmets for everyone on bikes, ATVs and snowmobiles, and spark arresters on dirt bikes and ATVs. Riders under 18 need a parent or guardian on site. No riding drunk or high. No driving in the river, along its banks or through streams. Cross on the bridge. Riders sign a waiver and need insurance.

Rent a snowmobile or quad

We rent snowmobiles and quads (ATVs) for $500 per machine per day, with any stay, even day use. You need insurance that covers riding, your own or through us (ask us about it). The rider signs a liability waiver, wears a helmet and rides where we show you. You pay for any damage or loss.

Motorcycle trips

I-80 over Donner Summit and Highway 49 through Gold Country are both close. Park your bikes by the cabin. After a cold ride over the pass, the sauna is right there.

Tents, vans and RVs

You can pitch tents or park vans and RVs near the cabin if you check with us first. Public campgrounds in the mountains book up months ahead.

A normal booking includes the cabin, the sauna, the grounds around them and the riverfront by the clearing. Stay 3 nights or more and the whole 168 acres opens up. Tell us your plans in the notes when you book.

Questions

How many people, and what does it cost?+

For up to 10 people, kids included, the rental is $500 for day use (noon to 9 p.m.) or $1,000 per night, plus a $250 cleaning fee once per booking. For 11 to 15 people it's $750 day use or $1,500 per night, plus the same $250 cleaning fee. 15 is the maximum.

Can we stay a month or longer?+

Yes. The calendar on this page books up to 7 nights. For a month or longer, message us on WhatsApp and we'll work out the price and terms with you.

Do we need 4WD or chains?+

Not in summer and fall. The road is paved from the gate to the cabin. In winter the road isn't plowed, so you park near the gate and snowshoe the last two-thirds of a mile. On I-80, chain controls start at Kingvale, a few miles past our exit if you're coming from Sacramento.

Is there cell service or Wi-Fi?+

No Wi-Fi or internet. Cell service is spotty, and people say AT&T works best. Tell someone your plans before you go.

Is the river OK for kids?+

The swimming hole is calm on top, but there's current underneath and no lifeguard. Kids need life jackets. People swim from mid-July through October. During spring runoff, May to early July, the river is dangerous and nobody swims.

Can we bring our dog?+

Yes, outdoors only and under your control. Dogs can't go in the cabin or the sauna.

Can we have a campfire?+

In winter, while there's snow on the ground around the cabin, yes, on the grounds away from buildings, cars and trees. The rest of the year, only in the fire spot we show you. Never during a burn ban, fire restrictions or a Red Flag warning (most years late June through October). Put it out cold. No fireworks.

Are there quiet hours? Can we play music?+

No quiet hours. Music is fine. There are no neighbors nearby, so nobody's going to come over and complain.

Can you hear the freeway?+

At the gate, yes, you can hear I-80. At the cabin it depends on the wind and the river.

What if I-80 is closed?+

If I-80 is officially closed on your check-in day, or there's an evacuation order, we'll move your stay once, to any open date within 12 months.

Will anyone be there when we arrive?+

No. Nobody lives on site and there's no staff. The cabin and sauna will be clean and the firewood stacked. If anything is dirty or broken, send us a photo within the first hour.
