How it works

  1. Arrival

    In summer and fall you drive right up to the cabin. In winter you leave the car by the road at the gate and walk the last two-thirds of a mile on snowshoes: nobody plows the snow here.

  2. While you're here

    The cabin and the sauna are clean and the firewood is stacked. You light the stoves in the sauna and the living room yourselves.

  3. Before you leave

    Clean up after yourselves and take your trash with you.

  4. The key

    Lock the cabin and leave the key where we tell you. We return the deposit in full. If we have to clean up or fix something after you, that comes out of the deposit.

Yoga and retreats

You can come here for yoga, for a retreat, or just to spend some quiet time by the river. You rent the whole place, so there's no one here but your group and the schedule is your own. A retreat can run for several days in a row, for a group of up to 15. After practice there's the wood-fired sauna and the river. There's no internet. Bring your own program, teacher and mats.

Walks and hikes

You can walk straight from the cabin: there are 168 acres of private forest around it, and the river runs through the property. The Loch Leven Lakes trail starts a mile from the exit: three and a half miles uphill to granite lakes, with a season of roughly June to November. The Pacific Crest Trail crosses I-80 at Donner Summit, fifteen minutes east.

Fishing

There are brown and rainbow trout in the South Yuba. Trout season in California runs from late April to mid-November, and from age 16 you need a fishing license.

Dirt bikes and quads

There's room to ride on 168 acres of private land, and a paved area by the cabin to unload. We'll agree on where to ride beforehand. California requires a spark arrester on forest land and Placer County limits noise, so bring quiet exhausts.

Motorcycle touring

I-80 over Donner Summit and Highway 49 through Gold Country are close by. You park the bikes right by the cabin and warm up in the sauna after a cold pass.

Camping

Tents, vans and RVs can go on the land, by arrangement. Public campgrounds in the mountains book up months ahead, and here there'll be no one but your group.

You book the cabin and the sauna, but the forest and the river around them are yours too. Write what you have in mind in the notes when you book, and we'll tell you if it works.

Questions

How many people can come?+

Up to 15, children included. The price is for the whole place, not per person: up to 10 people, $500 for a day and $1,000 for 24 hours; 11 to 15 people, $750 and $1,500.

Can we rent it for a month?+

Yes, the whole property, for a month or longer. We agree on the price and terms separately: message us on Facebook.

Do I need a 4WD or chains?+

Not in summer and fall: the road is paved from the gate to the cabin. In winter you can't drive past the gate, and on I-80 chain controls start at Kingvale, a few miles east.

Is there phone signal or Wi-Fi?+

There's no Wi-Fi or internet at the place, only mobile signal. Coverage is patchy; people say AT&T works best. Before you drive up, leave the address and your plans with someone.

Is the river safe for kids?+

It's a mountain river: the water is ice-cold, and there's current under the calm surface. In spring runoff, from May to early July, it's dangerous and nobody swims. In summer the pool below the deck is quiet, but kids need life jackets.

Can we bring a dog?+

Yes, but dogs stay outside: they aren't allowed in the cabin or in the sauna.

Can we make a fire?+

Yes, outside fire season. From late June to October open campfires are banned here and across the Sierra. Then you still have the sauna stove, the wood stove in the living room and the grills on the deck.

Can you hear the freeway?+

The exit is a couple of minutes away, so at the gate you can. How much reaches the cabin and the water depends on the wind and the river.
